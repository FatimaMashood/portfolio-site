## Introduction

The "Mapping Life and Landscapes, Past and Future" exhibition at the MU Hybrid Art House presents an exploration of the different ways that art, science and technology interact. The works present narratives that explore our relationship with the environment, with our bodies, with our history as well as the potential of technological and research innovation.

## Reach

<figure class="inline-figure is-wide">
  <img src="/images/elsewhere/exhibit-mu-reach-1200.webp" alt="Laparoscopic surgical instruments clamped around a lit tray on a dark table, under a desk lamp." loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Reach, Karel van Laere, MU Hybrid Art House</figcaption>
</figure>

Karel van Laere's "Reach" presents a multimedia (audio-visual) exploration of improvisation. The artist, a surgeon by profession, curated the work as an artefact of his curiosity for understanding how individuals improvise in different domains. While working with laparoscopic instruments, the artist presents a live sound installation to showcase the experience of improvisation. The artwork invites reflection on imagining processes such as improvisation or collaboration in different contexts. This research inspired me to think about the different kinds of processes/acts that we as individuals perform intuitively.

## re-c(O)unting

<figure class="inline-figure is-wide">
  <img src="/images/elsewhere/exhibit-mu-artefacts-1200.webp" alt="A curved white table holding shells, bones, glass bottles and an open case of specimen slides." loading="lazy" />
  <figcaption>Artefacts from the performance, re-c(O)unting, ella hebendanz, pamela varela and Joris Koene, MU Hybrid Art House</figcaption>
</figure>

Through an essay film and a live performance, the work explores the impact of the colonial medical approaches on female-assigned bodies. For me, the work showcases the ethical constraints involved in the interplay of art, science, and technology. The essay film along with the display of a variety of artefacts that were used in the performance.

The artists aim to deconstruct binaries that have existed for years between nature and technology, science and spirituality, as well as the male-female binary. The essay film started with a mention of three women whose bodies were used for science, without their consent. While the essay film mentioned the names of women whose bodies were used for science, it intrigued me into wanting a more intersectional view on the topic embodied with their narratives as opposed to an overview.

This work inspired me to ponder over Donna Haraway's proposal for deconstructing binaries/blurring them (Haraway, 1985) through a technologically mediated approach. I was intrigued to find that the performance led by the artists was positioned in a way that reflects an anatomical theatre, as a metaphor for the contentious relationship between anatomists and ethics.

## Conclusion

The works presented explored the different complexities and intricacies of the entanglements between art, science, and technology in contemporary society. I was drawn to the fact that Samsung was an official partner for this exhibit and a similar exhibit at Nxt Museum, also focusing on the interplay between art, science, and technology. My recent visit to the Nxt Museum (Amsterdam) exhibit, also showcasing the intersection of art, science and technology, inspired me to think about the mechanisms by which artworks are selected and by whom. For me, both exhibits, presented in partnership with Samsung, focused more on engaging the audience and less on presenting thought-provoking artistic inquiry.
